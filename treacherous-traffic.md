# A Priest and Pirates Spread the Myth
---

## 1602 Viscaíno Expedition as a Pivot Point

* Inaccuracies about the geography of California appeared in the early seventeenth century after the 1602 expedition of Sebastián Vizcaíno, sent by the Spanish to map the California coast.
* A priest on the expedition, Antonio de la Ascensión, wrote an account of the voyage describing the Sea of Cortez continuing into a northern strait, creating a passage to Europe.
* Europeans had long believed in the existence of a northwest ocean passage that connected the Atlantic to the Pacific, so were primed and eager to accept this account.

<img width="300" alt="Title page of Descripcion des Indes Occidential (Description of the East Indies)" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/03dac144-c307-4e75-812a-1ecd09720202" />

Herrera y Tordesillas, Antonio de. *DESCRIPTIO INDIÆ OCCIDENTALIS Per Antonium De Herrera Regium Indiarum Et Castellæ Historiographum*. s.n. [1622]
  
* First map to show California as an island on the title page of a 1622 Spanish atlas, *Descripcion des Indes Occidential (Description of the East Indies)*, by Spanish historian Antonio de Herrera y Tordesillas.
* The map shows California separated from the main North American continent, with a clear ocean passage above North America extending from California to the Atlantic Ocean.

## Pirates!


<img width="776" height="680" alt="First printed map in English to show California as an island" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8ba592f6-ab14-4d6e-8fb1-a115d686ad4a" />

* A Spanish ship carrying one of Ascensión's maps back to Spain was intercepted by Dutch pirates.
* The map made its way to England, where it was copied and published in 1625 by mathematician Henry Briggs, who considered it new cartographic intelligence.
* Note the inscription on the map: *“California: sometimes supposed to be part of the western continent, but since by a Spanish chart taken by the Hollanders, it is found to be a goodly island.”*
* Note the large title cartouche covering the Pacific Northwest where the map maker lacks geographic information
